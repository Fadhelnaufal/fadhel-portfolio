# Product

<!-- impeccable:product-schema 1 -->

## Platform

Web

## Product Name

Fadhel — Software Engineer Portfolio

## Users

### Primary Users

**Recruiters and hiring managers** evaluating Fadhel for frontend, UI engineering, or software engineering opportunities.

They are typically short on time and need to quickly understand:

* who Fadhel is;
* what he builds;
* the quality of his work;
* his technical capabilities;
* his professional experience;
* how he approaches product and engineering problems.

### Secondary Users

**Potential freelance clients and collaborators** looking for a developer who can design and build polished, production-oriented web applications.

They should be able to quickly identify relevant projects, capabilities, technologies, and ways to get in contact.

## Product Purpose

The portfolio is a professional digital representation of Fadhel as a software engineer.

Its primary purpose is not simply to list skills or experience, but to demonstrate engineering ability through the quality of the product itself.

The website should communicate:

* strong frontend engineering;
* thoughtful UI/UX implementation;
* modern web development practices;
* attention to detail;
* technical depth;
* product thinking;
* reliability and professionalism.

The portfolio itself should function as evidence of the capabilities it claims.

## Positioning

A premium personal portfolio for a software engineer who designs and builds modern digital products.

The portfolio should feel like a carefully crafted product rather than a conventional resume website.

Its visual identity should communicate:

* technical confidence;
* refined taste;
* precision;
* maturity;
* creativity;
* restraint;
* professionalism.

The website should avoid generic developer-portfolio conventions and avoid looking like an AI-generated template.

## Operating Context

The portfolio will primarily be accessed through:

* desktop computers;
* laptops;
* mobile phones;
* modern Chromium, Safari, and Firefox browsers.

Visitors may arrive with limited time and should be able to understand Fadhel's professional profile quickly without sacrificing depth for visitors who want to explore further.

## Core Product Principles

### 1. Show, Don't Tell

The quality of the portfolio itself should demonstrate Fadhel's frontend and UI engineering capabilities.

Prefer evidence over claims.

Show:

* real projects;
* real interfaces;
* meaningful technical decisions;
* actual outcomes;
* thoughtful interaction design.

Avoid excessive self-promotional statements without supporting evidence.

### 2. Craft Over Decoration

Visual sophistication should come from:

* typography;
* spacing;
* composition;
* hierarchy;
* imagery;
* interaction;
* consistency;
* intentional details.

Do not use visual effects merely because they are technically possible.

### 3. Accessible by Default

Accessibility is a fundamental product requirement, not an optional enhancement.

The implementation should follow WCAG principles and include:

* semantic HTML;
* keyboard accessibility;
* visible focus states;
* appropriate color contrast;
* meaningful labels;
* reduced-motion support;
* accessible navigation;
* appropriate image alternatives.

### 4. Performance Is Part of the Product

Performance is itself evidence of engineering quality.

The site should prioritize:

* fast initial loading;
* optimized images;
* efficient fonts;
* minimal unnecessary JavaScript;
* sensible animation;
* responsive rendering;
* Core Web Vitals;
* efficient Next.js patterns.

Target excellent Lighthouse performance without introducing artificial optimizations that compromise usability or maintainability.

### 5. Restraint

The design should be visually distinctive without becoming visually noisy.

Avoid:

* excessive gradients;
* unnecessary glassmorphism;
* decorative blobs;
* excessive shadows;
* generic dashboard aesthetics;
* unnecessary 3D effects;
* excessive animation;
* visual effects that distract from the work.

### 6. Authenticity

Only use genuine information, projects, experience, skills, and technologies.

Do not fabricate:

* clients;
* achievements;
* metrics;
* project outcomes;
* employment;
* technical experience;
* testimonials.

## Visual Direction

The visual language should feel:

* premium;
* editorial;
* modern;
* minimal;
* sophisticated;
* personal;
* technically precise.

Typography and composition should carry much of the visual identity.

The design should feel closer to a high-end editorial/product presentation than a generic developer landing page.

The portfolio should avoid the visual language commonly associated with AI-generated websites.

## Hero Direction

The hero is the primary visual statement of the portfolio.

The intended composition includes a strong typographic treatment of the name "Fadhel" combined with a portrait that visually emerges from the central composition.

The portrait should feel integrated into the typography and layout rather than appearing as a conventional profile-card image.

The hero should immediately communicate:

1. identity;
2. professional role;
3. visual quality;
4. personality;
5. confidence.

The hero should remain understandable and usable without relying on animation.

## Content Strategy

The information architecture should prioritize:

1. Hero
2. Selected Work
3. Capabilities
4. About / How I Work
5. Experience
6. Technology
7. Contact
8. Footer

Selected Work should receive significant visual emphasis because projects provide the strongest evidence of engineering ability.

## Project Presentation

Projects should not be presented as a simple grid of decorative cards.

Each selected project should communicate:

* project identity;
* purpose/problem;
* Fadhel's role;
* relevant technologies;
* meaningful implementation details;
* visual evidence;
* project outcome when factual information is available.

The presentation should prioritize quality and storytelling over the number of projects shown.

## Technical Constraints

The project uses:

* Next.js;
* React;
* TypeScript;
* Tailwind CSS;
* Vercel deployment.

Prefer:

* Server Components by default;
* minimal client-side JavaScript;
* semantic HTML;
* reusable components;
* typed content;
* maintainable architecture;
* optimized assets.

## Quality Targets

The finished product should aim for:

* excellent Lighthouse performance;
* strong Core Web Vitals;
* WCAG-compliant accessibility;
* responsive behavior across common screen sizes;
* clean semantic markup;
* maintainable TypeScript;
* production-ready deployment.

Specific numerical targets should be treated as quality goals rather than reasons to compromise UX or engineering quality.

## Interaction Principles

Motion should support:

* hierarchy;
* orientation;
* continuity;
* feedback;
* storytelling.

Motion should never exist merely to make the site appear impressive.

Respect:

```css
prefers-reduced-motion
```

Interactions should remain understandable and usable without animation.

## Out of Scope for V1

The first version should not include:

* CMS;
* authentication;
* user accounts;
* unnecessary dashboards;
* complex backend infrastructure;
* artificial AI features;
* excessive interactive experiments;
* features that do not directly strengthen the portfolio's professional purpose.

## Success Criteria

The portfolio succeeds when a visitor can quickly answer:

* Who is Fadhel?
* What does he build?
* What is he good at?
* What projects has he worked on?
* How does he approach engineering?
* What technologies does he use?
* How can I contact him?

At the same time, the visual and technical quality of the website should provide evidence that Fadhel can build polished, production-quality digital products.
